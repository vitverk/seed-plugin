## Seedlings

*Everything about Seedlings: needs, mood, health, skills, relationships, scheduling, and daily care.*
Seedlings are human clones with their own agency and autonomy. They are living, breathing organisms that grow, age, work, socialize, and eventually die, making their own choices within the world you help shape. Seedlings can live up to 120 years but may die earlier from starvation, thirst or severe untreated health conditions. Each Seedling has needs, a mood, skills, stats, jobs, relationships, interests, perks and more: each of which influences their independent decisions.
PAM doesn’t have access to your Seedling’s data. But you can find all their skills, interests, perks, and stats in Focus Mode. For mood, needs, thoughts, status effects, and what's hiding in their pockets, check out the Seedling Card.

### Deleting a Seedling and Starting Over
<!-- keywords: delete character remove seedling start over restart reset new life dies death -->
*Why Seedlings cannot be deleted, and how to start over with a new Seedling instead.*
Seedlings cannot be deleted 😣 they can die from neglection, but that is truly a cruel thing to do, Cultivator! A Seedling's name is also permanent: once chosen during Seedling creation it cannot be changed, so pick carefully at creation. Babies are the exception: a newborn arrives already carrying a given name, and you can change it while it is still an infant, either from the birth notification or from the crib's context menu. Whatever name the crib carries when the infant grows up becomes that Seedling's permanent name.
If you don't like the society you join and want to start over, you have two options. Migrate there with an existing Seedling using a Migration Ticket. Or, in World View, click the other society and select "Visit" to enter Visitor Mode, then use the "Start New Life" button to spawn a fresh Seedling directly into that society, no Migration Ticket needed: this requires a free Seedling Slot and a Seedling Life, plus room in that society's population. The "+" button used elsewhere always creates a Seedling in your current society; it's the Visitor Mode "Start New Life" button specifically that lets you start in a different one. Every new Seedling costs a Seedling Life. If your last Seedling dies, you get a Seedling Life for free (see "When Your Last Seedling Dies").

### When Your Last Seedling Dies
<!-- keywords: died dead death only seedling lost all my seedlings free new seedling life do I have to pay real money buy again game over emergency podster -->
*What happens when your only living Seedling dies: you get a free Seedling Life to keep playing.*
When your last living Seedling dies, you get a free Seedling Life. You do not need to buy one or spend real money to keep playing. You get this free Seedling Life every time your last Seedling dies.
You get it only when you have no other living Seedling, in any society. If another of your Seedlings is still alive, you continue playing with that Seedling.
The death notice pop-up shows a section called "A New Seedling Life". Click "To Character Creator" in that pop-up to create your new Seedling with the free Seedling Life.
If you close the pop-up instead, the free Seedling Life stays in your Cultivator Inventory, in your Cultivator Profile. It is used the next time you create a Seedling.

### Memoria
<!-- keywords: graveyard cemetery memorial passed away deceased obituary who died see my dead seedlings -->
*Memoria, the window with a record of each of your dead Seedlings: opening it, who is listed, what a record shows, and the Family view.*
Memoria keeps a record of your Seedlings that have died. It opens from the Memoria icon in the dock on the right side of the screen, the icon with the sunset. The icon appears only once at least one of your Seedlings has died.
Memoria lists only your own dead Seedlings, from every society you play in on this world. Seedlings that belong to other Cultivators are never listed, even when they are related to your Seedlings. When a Seedling passes away, its Spark profile posts that it has passed away. On Spark you can learn that another Cultivator's Seedling has died (see "Spark").
The list on the left shows a card for each Seedling, with its name and age at death. The newest death comes first. Type in "Search by name" to find one. A Seedling that died a moment ago is added to the list by itself shortly after.
"Continue your legacy" sits at the bottom of the list. It shows how many living Seedlings and Seedling Slots you have. Its button opens the SEED Store. A dead Seedling frees its Seedling Slot (see "Seedling Slots"). A dead Seedling's belongings move to your Cultivator Inventory (see "Seedling Inventory").

#### The Memoria Record
<!-- keywords: what killed how did my seedling die biography life summary missing empty blank -->
Select a Seedling in the list to open its record on the right.
- "In memory": a short obituary of the Seedling's life.
- "Seedling Highlights": click "Play" for a short recap of the life in numbers. "Replay" plays it again.
- Cards with "Lived" (its age), "Home" (its society), "Years lived", "Cause of death", and counts of "Jobs held", "Relationships", "Perks" and "Life stages".
- "Stats" and "Skills": how the Seedling was at the time of death.
- "Perks": the perks it developed, with "Interests pursued" below them. A Seedling without perks says "No perks developed."
- "Work": the jobs it held, each with how many years it lasted.
- "Close to": its partner, best friends and friends.
- "Life": switch between "Story", the life told in prose, and "Timeline", a list of moments such as arriving, moving, jobs, friendships, rivals and selfies. "View on Spark" opens the Seedling's Spark profile.
- "Last thoughts": the thoughts the Seedling had at the end.
Some records leave out sections. "Stats", "Skills" and "Interests pursued" appear only for Seedlings that died after Memoria began recording them. "Work", "Close to" and "Last thoughts" appear only when there is something to show. "Life" says "No recorded moments" when the Seedling lived before Spark kept memories. For those Seedlings, the "Relationships" card shows "—". They also have no "Close to".

#### Family View in Memoria
<!-- keywords: family tree relatives mother father son daughter spouse husband wife kids -->
In the list on the left, switch "Seedlings" to "Family". It shows the family of the Seedling you selected, as it was when that Seedling died. Parents are shown above the Seedling, children below, and siblings and partner beside it. The Seedling whose family you are looking at is marked "This record". Every relative shows "Living" or "Died at" an age. A relative that belongs to another Cultivator always shows "Living", even after it has died. A relative that had already died, or had moved away, before this Seedling died is not shown. Click a relative that has a record to open that record. A living relative cannot be opened. Switch back to "Seedlings" and pick another Seedling to see a different family. "No family recorded for" a Seedling means it had no parents, siblings, partner or children with it when it died, or that it died before Memoria recorded family.

### A Newborn: The Infant Crib
<!-- keywords: grow older age crying cry kid -->
*What a newborn is, when it grows up, and how to care for it.*
A newborn Seedling spends its first days as a baby in an Infant Crib, placed in the home of the Seedling who carried the pregnancy. Until the baby is [world-dependent value: infant becomes character real days] Earth days old, you can't see it in Focus Mode, so count from the birth message. Then it leaves the crib as a child in Early Childhood, you get a message, and you can see it in Focus Mode.
A baby has three needs: Food, Cuddle and Play. When one gets critical, the baby cries. Your Seedlings can feed it, cuddle it and play with it. Click the crib to pick one of these, or give a Seedling who lives in the baby's home the Address Baby's Need priority in the Scheduler. There you choose one need or any need, and a slider sets how low the need must be before your Seedling steps in.
This care also decides whether the baby keeps a trait it inherited, as described in Personality Traits.

### Seedling Card
*Bottom-left panel showing a Seedling's activity, mood, money, thoughts, and needs.*
The Seedling Card is your central hub for quickly understanding and managing the current state of an individual Seedling. It appears in the bottom left of your screen in the Society View and shows the Seedling you are currently playing. The Seedling Card consists of the following information:
Current Activity: Shows what the Seedling is doing right now (e.g., working, resting, eating).
Mood Indicator: Shows their current mood state, which is set by their active Thoughts.
SeedCoin Balance: Displays how much money this Seedling has in their Wallet App.
Locate Button: You can find your Seedling wherever they are, by clicking on this button
The Seedling card also contains four tabs:
Thoughts: short expressions that hint at what the Seedling is thinking or desiring.
Needs Summary: Color-coded icon bars showing the status of their various needs.
Status Effects: These show all the conditions/effects that are currently on your Seedling.
Seedling Inventory: A temporary storage space that Seedlings have access to.
The + next to the Seedling Card shows your Seedling Slots and lets you add a Seedling (see "Getting More Seedlings: Slots, Lives and Babies").

### Seedling Inventory
<!-- keywords: carry pocket bag dead loot inventory drop dies death orbital storage clothing teleported spaceport borrowed tools -->
*The Seedling's carried-item storage: capacity, picking up items, and placing objects.*
The Seedling Inventory is a temporary storage space accessible to Seedlings. Through Direct Control, you can instruct Seedlings to pick up items from the ground, their inventory, or other storage containers. Haul moves items into storage, never into the Seedling Inventory, so use Pick Up to have them carry something. The Seedling Inventory has a total capacity of [world-dependent value: character inventory volume] volume units. Some equippable items, such as backpacks and other bags, increase this capacity while worn.
Seedlings will carry these items until they are instructed to drop them off into a designated storage container. Please note: Seedlings have a strong preference for order. Dropping items on the ground creates total chaos, which they dislike, and is currently not possible, except for placeable objects. To place a placeable object, simply select it from you Seedling Inventory and you will enter Placement Mode.
When your Seedling dies, their inventory and the clothing they wore move to your Cultivator Inventory automatically. Borrowed tools stay behind as a pile where the Seedling died. Hauled resources stay in that pile too. You find the moved items in the Cultivator Inventory of your Cultivator Profile. You can hand them to any of your living Seedlings from there.

### Status Effects
<!-- keywords: plus minus buff debuff stat change icon strength timer duration expires meal food boost temporary -->
*Conditions and effects currently on a Seedling and how they change their Stats.*
Status effects indicate your Seedling's current condition and how it affects their Stats. These effects come from various sources, including satisfied or unmet needs, health conditions or injuries, and your Seedling's Perks. You can hover over a status effect to see where it comes from.
Some effects are temporary and run out on their own — a boost from one of the more involved cooked dishes, a layer of sunscreen, or the weakness that follows being unconscious. A cooked dish raises either one Stat or how fast a Seedling performs a particular kind of work. Timed effects appear as small icons on the Seedling Card, each with a fill that drains as the effect wears off. Hover one to see its name, what it changes, and how much time is left. When more than three are running at once, the rest sit behind a counter you can click.

### Health Conditions
*How illnesses and injuries work: severities, remedies, worsening, and self or doctor treatment.*
A health condition is a negative state that affects your Seedling's body and abilities. Conditions come in two kinds: injuries (like burns, cuts, and strains) make a Seedling Injured, and illnesses (like stomach trouble, coughs, or eye problems) make them Sick. Each condition affects a specific body part, reduces your Seedling's Stats while it is active, and usually brings on a negative thought that weighs on their mood. If left untreated, many conditions can worsen over time, and the most serious can become life-threatening.

You can see your Seedling's health conditions in the Seedling Card, and in Focus Mode's Body tab. There you can inspect each condition in detail: its severity, what caused it, which Stats it is dragging down, how long it lasts, whether it can worsen or kill, and how to treat it. Every condition's exact remedy is listed in the Health Conditions Reference.

#### Checking Other Cultivators' Seedlings
<!-- keywords: hurt wounded ill someone else neighbor neighbour not my seedling -->
*Seeing when Seedlings that aren't yours are sick or injured.*
If a Seedling that isn't yours is sick or needs help, a tag appears above them in the world. It shows Injured or Sick only when a condition is Severe. You can help treat them. Just make sure your Seedling has the right remedy in their inventory or in a nearby container. If you don't see the tag, check that Floating notifications is turned on in the visibility settings (eye icon) at the bottom right of the screen.

#### Where Conditions Come From
Health conditions arise in three ways:

Accidents at work: Seedlings can hurt themselves when they fail at demanding activities. Working at a furnace or anvil can cause burns; workbenches and sharp tools can cause cuts; heavy physical work can cause strains; mining or working at deposits can irritate the eyes or chest; and handling chemicals at a chemistry station can make a Seedling sick. The harder the activity and the lower your Seedling's relevant skill, the more likely they are to fail and the worse the result.

Contaminated food and drink: Some consumables can make a Seedling sick. Drinking unpurified Murky Water can cause Jaundice, and eating raw mushrooms or raw algae can cause Indigestion. Purify water and cook food before consuming to stay safe. Seedlings only drink Murky Water or eat raw mushrooms, Golden Caps or raw algae on their own when they are starving or parched. You can still tell them to.

Too much sun can also be bad for a Seedling and give them sunburn, so don't forget the sunscreen!

#### Condition Severity
Every health condition has one of three severity levels: Minor, Moderate, or Severe. Severity determines how much it impacts your Seedling's Stats, how long it lasts, whether it is life-threatening, and what kind of treatment it needs.

Minor conditions are the least dangerous, affecting Stats only slightly. They can usually be treated by the Seedling themselves.

Moderate conditions affect Stats more noticeably. Some can still be self-treated, but most need a doctor.

Severe conditions are critical and can be life-threatening. They almost always require a skilled doctor and a specific remedy.

#### How Conditions Worsen and Heal
An untreated condition can progress to a higher severity (Minor to Moderate to Severe), and a Severe condition can ultimately be fatal. Worsening happens in two ways: a condition may deteriorate as its timer runs out, or flare up worse if the Seedling is exposed to its cause again. The best protection is to treat conditions early, before they escalate. Treatment tilts the outcome in the Seedling's favour: some remedies remove the condition outright, while most shorten how long it lasts and greatly improve the odds that it heals instead of worsening when its timer runs out.

After recovering from some health conditions, a Seedling gains temporary immunity, so they won't catch that same condition again for a while.

#### Treatment
<!-- keywords: doctor heal cure -->
Remedies are applied in one of two ways:

Self-treatment: For minor and some moderate conditions, the Seedling can treat themselves by using the right remedy. They will head to a suitable spot and apply it on their own. No help needed, just a remedy within reach.

Doctor treatment: More serious conditions must be treated by another Seedling acting as a doctor, who performs a treatment activity on the patient using the required remedy. A doctor's medical skills affect how well the treatment goes (see Medical Skills).

Not every treatment works in a single step. Some remedies clear a condition in one application; others are an ongoing course that must be re-applied over time to keep the condition from worsening; and the most serious conditions allow only one decisive procedure: a single chance to improve the odds that the condition heals instead of worsening while it runs its course. A condition's detail panel in Focus Mode always tells you which remedy it needs and whether your Seedling can apply it themselves or needs a doctor.

##### Treating a Condition, Step by Step
1. **Find out what the condition needs.** Open the condition in Focus Mode's Body tab: its Condition Details name the remedy that treats it at its current severity and show whether your Seedling can apply it themselves.
2. **Get the remedy within reach.** Craft it at a Medical Fabricator (a Seedling with the Pharmacy skill produces it), buy it from the Marketplace like any other item, or take it from a container that already has one. For self-treatment, the remedy needs to be in your Seedling's inventory or in a container they can access.
3. **Self-treatment happens on its own.** A Seedling with a treatable condition and a matching remedy in reach will treat themselves autonomously. You can also trigger it directly: click your Seedling and choose "Apply from Inventory", or click a nearby container holding the remedy and pick the treatment option there.
4. **For doctor treatment, bring the two together.** Select the Seedling acting as doctor and click the patient to choose "Give treatment" — or, playing as the patient, click the doctor and choose "Ask for treatment". The doctor will fetch the required remedy from nearby storage if needed, and the menu shows the chance the treatment succeeds, which grows with the doctor's medical skills. Doctor-only remedies cannot be self-applied, no matter who holds them.
5. **Or let a clinic handle it.** A business can advertise healing as a service (see Service Locations): hire a Seedling as the doctor with a healing work priority, stock the remedies, and Seedlings who need treatment will come visit on their own — including yours, who will seek out such a location autonomously when they have a condition it can treat. A clinic only treats the conditions it offers healing for, not every kind of sickness. Your Seedling may also be turned away if the clinic has already hit its daily visitor limit, or if the treatment costs more than their daily budget.

If your society has no doctor and no clinic yet, minor and moderate conditions are usually still manageable through self-treatment — focus on getting the right remedy in reach. For a severe condition with nobody skilled enough to treat it, ask other Cultivators in [PlayersChat] for help.

#### Medical Skills
Treating health conditions draws on a family of medical skills, all part of the Medicine skill tree.

Medicine is the foundation: broad medical knowledge that opens up the rest of the field.

Self-treatment itself needs no skill: any Seedling can apply a remedy they have. First Aid (which a Seedling can begin once they've started Medicine) makes self-treatment faster, and it decides the success of basic remedies like Painkillers and Cough Syrup. Most other remedies succeed based on the matching specialist skill below, whether self-applied or given by a doctor.

Pharmacy is the other branch of Medicine: the craft of making the remedies themselves, preparing medicines from harvested and processed ingredients. A Seedling needs Pharmacy to produce remedies at the Medical Fabricator, and a higher level lets them craft faster and succeed more often. Every society that wants to stay healthy needs at least one Seedling working on this.

Once a Seedling reaches First Aid level 20, a set of specialist skills opens up. These are the doctor's craft, used to treat more serious conditions, and each covers a part of the body:
- Wound Care: severe cuts and wound infections
- Burn Care: severe burns
- Cardiology: heart and cardiovascular illnesses
- Gastroenterology: stomach, gut, and liver illnesses
- Nephrology: kidney (renal) illnesses
- Neurology: nerve and brain illnesses
- Ophthalmology: eye illnesses
- Dentistry: mouth and teeth illnesses
- Physiotherapy: strains
- Traumatology: sudden, severe traumatic injuries
- Infectiology: infections
- Fracture Care: broken bones and fractures

For all of these, a higher skill level raises the chance of a successful treatment. The most critical procedures, those using an IV Catheter or an Oxygen Mask, draw on the doctor's base Medicine skill instead. Note that a Seedling will only attempt a treatment on their own if they feel confident, and that confidence grows as the skill levels up. If their skill is still low, you can use Direct Control to have them attempt it anyway. Like every skill, medical skills improve through practice, so the more a Seedling treats the sick and injured, the better a healer they become.

One related skill sits outside the Medicine tree: Water Treatment lets a Seedling operate filtration units that turn murky water into clean water, a good way to prevent waterborne illness before it starts.

#### Remedies
A Remedy is a consumable item used to treat health conditions. Each remedy targets specific conditions and severities. Self-applied remedies (such as Painkillers, Antibiotics, Antacids, Bandages, Cooling Gel, Muscle Relaxant, Cough Syrup, and Eye Drops) can be used by the sick or injured Seedling themselves. Doctor-applied remedies (such as Splints, Surgical Repair Kits, Advanced Burn Treatment Kits, Anesthetic, IV Catheters, Defibrillators, and Oxygen Masks) must be administered by another Seedling.

Remedies are crafted from materials using schematics, primarily at the Medical Fabricator by a Seedling with the Pharmacy skill, and can also be purchased from the Marketplace or traded between Seedlings. Keep a supply stocked in nearby containers so your society is ready when illness or injury strikes.

### Focus Mode
*Detailed Seedling view with tabs for stats, skills, development, and body health.*
You can access Focus Mode by clicking the icon of a person with a focus frame in the bottom-right corner of the screen, or by clicking directly on your Seedling in the Seedling Card. This opens up a detailed view of your Seedling’s inner workings, organized into tabs: Development (interests, perks, personality traits, and life stages), Skills, and Body (health conditions and fertility), alongside their 12 Stats.

#### Stats
<!-- keywords: charisma intelligence strength agility stat which stats does an activity use good at farming success chance -->
Open up Focus Mode for a detailed view of your Seedling's current stats. Every Seedling is defined by a unique profile of 12 core Stats, 6 Mental and 6 Physical categories. These values represent the core capabilities of your Seedling. The initial stats are set during Seedling creation, where you allocate stat points across the Physical and Mental categories yourself. This foundational setup defines your Seedling's natural strengths and weaknesses from the very beginning.

##### How Stats affect your Seedling
- Attributes: Stats govern physical and mental traits, such as the Agility Stat, which determines a Seedling’s walking speed.

##### How Stats change
- Momentary Changes: These are temporary fluctuations caused by your Seedling’s current state. For example, low needs (like hunger or exhaustion) or specific ongoing activities may cause a temporary penalty to certain Stats.
- Permanent Changes: These are lasting improvements to a Seedling’s baseline capabilities. Permanent Stat boosts are typically earned through Perks, allowing you to specialize or improve your Seedling over the long term.

##### 12 Stats of a Seedling
- Agility: Related to control of the body in motion, impacting walking speed and physical activities.
- Charisma: Affects a Seedling’s ability to captivate others through performance and speech, influencing activities like dancing, singing, and public expression
- Creativity: Governs a Seedling’s ability to produce original artistic works, influencing activities like painting, sculpting, and writing
- Dexterity: Determines a Seedling’s precision and hand coordination, affecting activities like crafting and repairing
- Intelligence: Determines a Seedling’s ability to process complex information, impacting activities like crafting electronics, strategic games like chess, and treating injuries and illnesses with remedies
- Intuition: Reflects a Seedling’s instinctive understanding of subtle processes, influencing activities like baking, sculpting, and treating injuries and illnesses with remedies
- Memory: Determines how well a Seedling retains and recalls information, impacting the effectiveness of studying and knowledge-based activities
- Perception: Influences a Seedling’s attention to subtle detail, impacting artistic crafting and activities where precision and observation matter
- Resilience: Represents a Seedling’s physical toughness and resistance to strain, influencing injury risk and performance in demanding activities like weight lifting and mining
- Stamina: Represents a Seedling’s ability to sustain physical activity over time, impacting endurance-based activities like training calisthenics, silat, or training on monkey bars
- Strength: Measures a Seedling’s physical power, affecting activities like crafting, weight lifting, and gathering materials
- Willpower: Represents a Seedling’s mental discipline, influencing activities like studying and yoga that require sustained focus and self-control
These are only examples. Each activity uses its own skill and stats. To see them, hover over an activity in the Direct Control menu. Its Success Chance lists the skill and stats under Contributors. The Catalog's Activities tab shows them under "Chance modified by".

#### Life Stages
<!-- keywords: live lifespan age old die -->
Seedlings age naturally and go through 28 life stages. You can see their current age and life stage in Focus Mode, under the Development tab. By clicking on the life stages card, you can view detailed descriptions of each stage and the effects they have on your Seedling. For example, younger Seedlings may walk or learn faster. Planning your Seedling's activities around their life stage can help you get things done more efficiently. Seedlings can die of old age, dehydration, malnutrition, or a severe untreated health condition. Making sure that a Seedling's needs are always met will help them live a long and healthy life.
Child Seedlings, in every life stage before Young Adult, can't take a job.
Each life stage also introduces objectives tailored to the Seedling's personality, interests, and traits. These objectives must be fulfilled within the current life stage: when the Seedling ages into the next stage, any incomplete objectives are lost and their progression is not preserved.

#### Interests
Interests can be found in the Development Tab when you open up Focus mode. Interests are selectable lifestyles that unlock Perks for your Seedlings. During free time, Seedlings seek out activities that match their Interests on their own. After selecting an Interest, you must figure out how to satisfy an Interest which can then unlock Perks for your Seedling. For example, fulfilling a Builder Interest can unlock the Quick Fixes perk, which reduces repair time. There is no best Interest or Perk for everyone, it's totally up to you! Explore the associated perks of an interest in Focus Mode and choose the one that you desire the most.
To find activities that will satisfy your Seedling's Interests, check the Activity Categories listed in the Contributing Activity section on each Interest sheet, or open the Catalog's Activities tab and filter by the matching category, every contributing activity is clickable and links straight to its detail page. Make sure you take a closer look to the description on each sheet for more information as well. To help your Seedling develop a specific interest, send them to perform those activities. You can click on objects in the world to see all possible interactions and choose those that match the activity category. Alternatively, a simpler method is to use the Scheduler and setting Pursue Interest as their priority. The Seedling will then seek out activities available on the property that satisfy their interests during those scheduled times.

Your Seedling will then find where they can perform the activities.
Once interests are chosen for this life stage, there's no way to change it. Seize the time and satisfy the interest to unlock the perks. In the next Life Stage you will be able to pick new ones.

#### Perks
Perks are special traits that stay with your Seedling forever. Once you unlock a Perk, your Seedling keeps it permanently. These traits help define your Seedling's personality and improve their abilities. There are dozens of different Perks to collect. Each Interest has its own set of possible Perks. It is a good idea to check the full list of Perks for an Interest before you start, so you can help your Seedling grow in the direction you want.

##### How to get Perks
You earn Perks by completing Interests. In each Life Stage you can pick up to three Interests for your Seedling. Have your Seedling perform activities related to those Interests (see the contributing activities on each Interest's detail sheet). Use Direct Control to find objects where your Seedling can practice those activities, or set the Scheduler priority to "Pursue Interest." When an Interest completes, the game offers three random Perks linked to that Interest: you choose one to keep permanently.

#### Personality Traits
<!-- keywords: child none missing empty -->
Personality Traits define who your Seedling is and how they interact with the world. There are 25 traits in total. When you create a Seedling, you pick five of them yourself, shaping their permanent personality. A Seedling born in Avesta doesn't pick traits. It can inherit a trait from the Seedling who carried the pregnancy, but keeps it only if it got enough feeding, cuddling and play as a baby (see A Newborn: The Infant Crib). Without that care, it grows up with no personality traits. It still talks and builds relationships like any Seedling. The game picks a communication style for it at random, and that sets how it talks. With no traits on top, nothing else in its personality shapes its conversations, who it is attracted to, or which goals it chooses. Traits are tied to the Seedling's core identity and cannot be changed afterwards.
You will notice these traits most in how Seedlings communicate: they shape how your Seedling speaks with other Seedlings, how well they get along, and the tone of their messages to you.

#### Skills
<!-- keywords: learn skill faster study xp experience fail failing failed critical success multiple skills at once -->
You can explore Skills in the Focus Mode within the Skills tab. It consists of Skills and Schematics.
Seedlings unlock and level up skills by performing related activities. Some activities train several skills at once. Treating an ailment, for example, can build Physiotherapy, First Aid, and Medicine together. Outcome quality also affects the XP granted, and not always as you'd expect: for most activities a bad outcome trains the skill more than a routine success does, so an occasional botched attempt isn't wasted. Skills also level at different natural paces, so equal time spent won't always mean equal progress. Each skill sheet shows how to level it up. Its New Unlocks list shows which schematics the skill unlocks, and at which level. Higher skill levels make your Seedling faster and more successful at tasks, and can unlock new skills and schematics.

Seedlings can also study skill books to gain XP. Studying takes time, like any activity. With Practice Skill set in the Scheduler, a Seedling may also study a book for that skill if one is in storage they can reach. Novice books cover levels 1–10, Competent 11–20, Skilled 21–30; a Seedling can only read a book within their current level range. Once they outlevel it, the book still works for others. There is no book past level 30, so past Skilled, practicing is the only way to keep leveling. To make that practicing efficient without manually finding activities, use the Scheduler's Practice Skill priority (see Finding Activities to Practice Skills) so the Seedling automatically seeks out activities for the skill you pick.

##### Finding activities to practice skills
- Locate Activities Manually: Look around Avesta, move your camera, and click on objects to see what activities are available.
- Use the Scheduler: Go to the Scheduler and set Practice Skill as their activity priority. The Seedling will automatically seek out activities that will improve the selected target skill.

If you really don't find the right objects to interact with to improve skills, ask in Players Chat where you can do it.

#### Activity Outcomes
Some activities have different possible outcomes. A Seedling's relevant Skills and Stats both influence the likelihood of a positive outcome. Seedlings will avoid attempting activities where their chances of success are too low.

#### Schematics
<!-- keywords: recipe how to make learn get unlock schematic -->
Schematics are crafting instructions. A Seedling can craft with a schematic once their skill reaches the level it needs. The New Unlocks list on each skill sheet shows these levels (see Skills). They are valuable because once a Seedling knows a schematic, they can use it to craft items for you or any employer who hires them. You can use your Seedling's knowledge of cool schematics to impress potential employers in the Players Chat.

#### Body Tab (Health and Fertility)

#### In Crisis

*What "In crisis" means and which needs cause it.*
"In crisis" can mean two things. A need crisis happens when Water, Food, Rest, Energy or Bladder drops dangerously low, even while the Seedling is healthy. Focus Mode then shows a Crisis banner naming the need, and for Water and Food you also get a Crisis Warning. It ends once the need is refilled enough. #### Water Need
Water is a critical survival need which if ignored for too long, will lead to death. Seedlings must stay hydrated by consuming water or beverages from a storage container they have access to. To make your Seedling drink, click on a storage container that holds water and select Consume, then choose the water item. Seedlings can also drink from a water pile on the ground. Be careful: drinking Murky Water can make your Seedling sick! Use machines like water pumps and filtration units to produce clean water. Water can be extracted from the environment using machines that can pull water out of the ground or the air. Seedlings will autonomously prioritize finding something to drink, when their water need bar is low. (May not apply: nutrition enabled.) An unattended Seedling will hit a thirst crisis after roughly a week on Avesta, with stat penalties and unhappy thoughts: water depletes about twice as fast as food. Going without drinks for weeks empties the Hydration nutrient, and that is what can kill (see Nutrition). (Alternative; may not apply.) Thirst is tracked as a single bar, not a fluid volume, so "how much they drink" is whatever the bar needs at the moment.

#### Food Need
<!-- keywords: get food hungry eat -->
Food is a critical survival need which if ignored for too long, will lead to death. Seedlings must stay nourished by consuming food from a storage container or vending machine they have access to. Seedlings can also eat directly from a resource pile on the ground (for example, a freshly harvested mushroom pile, but be careful, raw mushrooms can make your Seedling sick!). To feed your Seedling from a storage container, click on the container and select Consume, then choose the food item. You cannot manually order a Seedling to eat from their personal inventory, but a hungry Seedling will eat food they are carrying on their own. Seedlings can eat food they've harvested, crafted, or bought from the Marketplace. Seedlings will autonomously prioritize finding something to eat, when their food need bar is low. (May not apply: nutrition enabled.) An unattended Seedling hits the desperate-hunger crisis well before any nutrient runs out, with stat penalties and unhappy thoughts. Going without food for months can empty the Protein nutrient, and that is what can kill (see Nutrition). (Alternative; may not apply.) An unattended Seedling can survive roughly two weeks on Avesta without food before dying, about twice as long as without water, but the desperate-hunger crisis kicks in well before that, with stat penalties and unhappy thoughts long before death. #### Rest Need
Rest is a functional need that should be maintained lest a Seedling will faint. Fainting is a short, harmless collapse from exhaustion: unlike falling Unconscious from (May not apply: nutrition enabled.) an empty Hydration or Protein nutrient (Alternative; may not apply.) an empty life-critical need, it needs no revival and passes on its own. Seedlings must stay rested using beds, cots, hammocks or other furniture objects that allow Seedlings to rest. These furniture objects can be crafted or bought. Alternatively, you can find places that offer 'Rest' activities using the Map View. You can also find these in the world by moving your camera around and clicking on the various things you see. Seedlings will autonomously prioritize finding a bed to sleep on, when their rest need bar is low. A full sleep in a bed (or sleeping tent) takes roughly 6 hours on Avesta and fully restores both Rest and Energy from empty, so 8 hours scheduled for sleep is more than enough on a normal day. Rest depletes more slowly than food or water, but skipping it still hurts mood and stats.

#### Energy Need
Energy is a functional need that should be maintained lest a Seedling will be drained. Seedlings recover Energy with short sitting breaks (a proper chair restores more than the bare ground) and, above all, by sleeping. Seedlings will autonomously prioritize finding a place to sit when their energy need bar is low. A full sleep restores Energy alongside Rest. Working, exercising, and studying drain Energy faster than passive time, so a Seedling on long shifts may need extra sitting breaks even after a good night's sleep.

Seedlings need regular bathroom access wherever they are. Toilets can be crafted using basic machines or can be bought from the Marketplace. Comfort is a wellbeing need that should be met in order to have a healthy Seedling with a stable mood. Comfort comes from relaxing environments, quality furniture and tasty foods. Soft seating, calming soundscapes, or enjoyable activities and food improve this need. Seedlings will autonomously prioritize finding a comfy object or food, when their comfort need bar is low.

### Relationships
<!-- keywords: fall in love marry married couple partner focus focuses -->
*How relationship statuses grow, couples form, breakups happen, and social plans work.*
Seedlings start as Acquaintances, and from there relationships grow on their own, driven by how the two Seedlings actually feel. You never assign or approve a relationship status — statuses follow automatically from Like and Romance values. Friendly feelings grow into Casual Friend, Friend, and Best Friend; negative feelings sink into Dislike, Rival, and even Enemy. These statuses are one-sided: your Seedling's best friend may not consider them theirs. Keeping a status is a little easier than earning it, and when feelings fall too low, the status doesn't drop immediately — you'll see a fading warning first, and the status only drops if the feelings don't recover in time.

Romance needs attraction. A Seedling with no attraction to another refuses anything romantic, and directing them yourself will not override it. Weak attraction can build up over time, so a slow start can still get there. A Seedling already in a couple relationship will not start romance with anyone else on its own, but you can direct them to do so.

Couples are the exception: Dating and Partner are always mutual and never form on their own. When your Seedling's feelings toward someone are strong enough, you can have them ask that Seedling to start dating: choose Speak in that Seedling's context menu, then pick "Ask ... to go out with you" under the Romantic topic. Once they are Dating, you can direct them to propose partnership. The ask plays out as a real conversation between the two Seedlings, and the asked Seedling answers immediately from its own feelings toward the asker; its Cultivator is not consulted and cannot veto the answer. A yes forms the couple right away; after a no, your Seedling cannot ask that Seedling again for a while. You can also end any non-family relationship: remotely from the Social App, or by directing your Seedling to break up in person, a public scene bystanders can witness. Breaking up hurts. Both Seedlings' feelings take a significant hit, and the relationship drops to whatever status the reduced feelings support before it can slowly regrow.

Seedlings autonomously schedule get-togethers with their close relationships at a place one of them liked going to before (a home, a service location or a society building), or else at one of their homes. Each Seedling has a weekly appointment limit, set in the Social app's settings, and every appointment counts toward it, including the ones you book and the ones it agrees to in conversation. Each day a Seedling may plan one get-together for that day or the next, more likely with someone it likes or loves more; over a week it plans about a quarter of its limit itself.

Seedlings can also make plans in conversation: one Seedling asks another to hang out — or, when there is a romantic spark, asks them out on a date — proposing a free two-hour window later in the week at a place one of them liked going to before (a home, a service location or a society building), or else at one of their homes. The other Seedling agrees or declines depending on how they feel about the asker and whether their calendar has room; an agreed plan lands in both Seedlings' calendars right away. You can also prompt this yourself by telling your Seedling in Seedling Chat to ask someone to hang out or to ask them out.

#### Calendar and Activity Priorities
<!-- keywords: plan day priority ignoring schedule -->
You use the Calendar to decide your Seedling's activity priorities at a selected time and selected location. You can select a variety of priorities for your Seedling, for example: Farm (plant, water and harvest crop fields); Harvest (trees, stones, berries and other wild resources, and Fetch Water); Haul (move items into storage, and carry your own deliveries); Produce; Practice Skill (this also covers studying a skill book); Pursue Interest; Address Need (like eating or sleeping); or Address Baby's Need.

You can observe the Activity Queue located above the Seedling Card to see which priorities your Seedling is currently performing. If you notice that a Seedling is performing an activity different from their set priority, it might be because they have some urgent physical need or that there is no possibility to perform the selected priorities. Take a close look at the environment they are at and remove the hurdles they encounter so that they can carry out that activity as desired.

A Seedling who keeps skipping a scheduled priority almost always has a good reason. Most often a need won't wait: hunger, rest and the other survival needs come before any scheduled task, and they pick it back up once they're settled. Sometimes the task itself can't run, because the production task hit its stop condition ("production limit reached", covered in the Crafting and Production Tasks section), ran out of materials, or has nowhere to put what it makes. ### Wallet

*App showing a Seedling's SeedCoin balance, transactions, and money transfers.*
This is one of the Apps on the Seedling's mobile device. You can use it to view your specific Seedling's SeedCoin balance and past transactions, and to send money. You can only send to recipients in your society. A Seedling can send money to:
- your other Seedlings;
- Seedlings they have a relationship with;
- businesses and departments whose wallet you are allowed to use;
- groups and other organizations they belong to, if their role lets them use its wallet;
- your society's government wallet.
A business can send money to other businesses in the society, to your Seedlings, and to the society. A group can send money only to its members. The society and its departments can send money to anyone in the society.
The Wallet app only ever shows the selected Seedling's own wallet. There is no switch to a business or society wallet inside it; business wallets appear there only as transfer recipients. ### Groups
<!-- keywords: resident invite residence invitation live together move in shared home accept invite -->
*App for forming Seedling groups that share property, chat, and SeedCoin.*
This is one of the Apps on the Seedling's mobile device. You can invite other Seedlings you have relationships with to a Group. This provides shared access to property (you can all live together and own a house together), chat, and SeedCoin transfers. Creating a group costs nothing: pick a name and your Seedling becomes its head. Your Seedling needs the Found organizations permission for groups. The default Adult and Minor roles have it unless the society removes it. A single Seedling can head up to 10 groups.
Groups are organizations with two fixed roles: one Group Head and Group Members (up to 100 Seedlings).(May not apply: organization browser enabled.)  The Group Head can also create custom roles on the group's Roles page. A group's role can have a schedule and a Daily Wage at a building the group administers. A group cannot hire Seedlings. Any member can invite new Seedlings from the Groups app, as long as the inviter has a relationship with them; an unanswered invitation expires after seven real-world days. The invite screen asks which role to invite them as - the plain Group Member role is the default, and a role beyond it can be offered only by those allowed to offer that role (see Invited Roles). An invitation needs no further approval: the invited Seedling just accepts or declines.
All members share access to group-owned property and resources, can use the group wallet, and can rename the group. Only the Group Head can sell or transfer group property, edit the group's jobs and contracts, and disband the group. Members leave on their own from the group's info screen.(May not apply: organization browser enabled.)  The Group Head can also remove a member with Remove on the group's Members page in the Organizations window. (Alternative; may not apply.)  There is no way to remove a member from a group.
A group can never be handed over to someone else. If the head dies or leaves the society, one of the remaining members automatically becomes the new head; a group with no members left is dissolved and its remaining funds go to the society. Disbanding by hand is only possible while the head is the group's last member and the group owns or administers no property. The money left in the group's wallet goes to the head who disbands it.(May not apply: organization browser enabled.)  Your groups also appear in the Organizations window under the Groups tile on the left of the window. You can also found a group there with the plus tile (New Organization), then Group.
Living together works through the group: a building the group administers is its shared home. A member with the right to manage that building's residents invites another member to live there from the building's Residents block in the Properties Panel (Invite, then pick the Seedling). The invited Seedling must already be a group member, so to move a friend in, invite them into the group first and send the residence invite once they have joined. The residence invite arrives in the Groups app under Pending Invites as a card reading "The group ... invites you to live here", separate from a plain membership invite(May not apply: organization browser enabled.) , and also in the inbox of the Organizations window; accepting moves the Seedling in right away and makes that building their home. An invite between two of your own Seedlings is accepted automatically. Only group-administered buildings use invitations: for a building administered by a Seedling or a business, the same Invite button moves the chosen Seedling in immediately.
